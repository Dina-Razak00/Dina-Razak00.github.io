About Me

Hey! I’m Dina, a game developer from Malaysia who loves cute things, cats, and pixel art; which makes it slightly ironic that my first full game turned out to be a psychological horror story.

I created Hey Jane! from the ground up, writing every line, directing its art, and testing each chapter until it was ready to publish. Working on the game showed me just how much I enjoy creating worlds, stories, and experiences that people can step into.

Before getting into game development, I worked in customer service and trained as a medical lab technician. Those experiences taught me patience, attention to detail, and the importance of understanding and listening to people.

Now, I’m looking for my first opportunity in the games industry; whether that’s an internship or junior role, where I can continue learning, grow my skills, and help create games that people enjoy.

Why I Changed Career Paths

For the past few years, I found myself working towards a career that I knew, deep down, wasn't making me happy. Eventually, I realised that I didn't want to keep putting off something I had always wanted to pursue. I wanted to build a career around something I genuinely cared about.

I've wanted to become a game developer since I was 16. For a long time, though, I convinced myself that programming wasn't for me. I thought being a programmer meant being naturally good at mathematics, which was never my strongest subject, so I put that dream aside and chose a different path.

As I've grown older, I've become more willing to challenge the assumptions that once held me back. With so many resources, courses, communities, and tools available online today, I realised I didn't need to have everything figured out before I started. I just needed to start learning.

My interest in games began through watching YouTubers and streamers play them and seeing how strongly people connected with the experiences they played. I became fascinated by the idea that something created by one person or a small team could bring people together, make them feel something, and become part of their lives.

That's what I want to create.

I'm still at the beginning of my journey, but I'm excited to keep learning, experimenting, and building towards the career I've wanted since I was 16.

How I made Hey Jane!

1. The idea

The idea behind my first game, Hey Jane!, came from a long-time dream of creating a horror/psychological experience. Inspired by creators like Chilla's Art and Kyle Seeley, the developer behind Emily is Away, I wanted to bring together the unsettling atmosphere of psychological horror with the emotional depth of interactive storytelling. And just like that, Hey Jane! was born.

2. Writing the story

I wrote all seven chapters and every line of dialogue. The whole story is told through Jane's computer: her chats, emails, journal and photos, so players piece together what's happening themselves.

3. The art

I created or art-directed all the pictures in the game: Jane's photos, journal doodles, creepy camera shots, and a short in-game video.

4. Building it in Unity

Hey Jane! takes place on a working 2003 desktop made in Unity 6, with a messenger, email, journal, web browser, photos and files. I designed how every app works and feels, planned each feature in detail, and tested every change in Unity until it played the way I imagined.

5. Testing

I played every chapter over and over, writing bug reports with steps to repeat them, and re-tested every fix before each release. A few of the bugs I caught:

  • Typing in a chat set off other buttons on the screen
  • Videos showing up completely black
  • Notifications stacking on top of each other
  • Keys that stopped responding

Player feedback shaped it too: a tester lost their progress by closing the game mid-chapter, so I added auto-saving, with a little walking ladybug while it saves.

6. Publishing

Hey Jane! is out on Unity Play, with a Windows version too. Because it deals with dark themes, I added a content warning and support helplines for Malaysia, the US and internationally.
